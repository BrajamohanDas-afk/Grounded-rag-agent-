Renewable Energy Policy Instruments

A feed-in tariff guarantees renewable generators a fixed price for every kilowatt-hour they export to the grid, usually for a contract period of 15 to 20 years. Germany's feed-in tariff, introduced in 2000, is widely credited with kick-starting the global solar market.

Net metering lets households with rooftop solar offset their consumption: the electricity meter effectively runs backwards when the home exports power, and the customer is billed only for net consumption over the billing period.

Renewable portfolio standards require utilities to source a minimum share of their electricity from renewable sources by a target date.
