# Grid Integration of Renewables

## Variability

Solar and wind output varies with weather and time of day. Grid operators describe this as variability, and it must be balanced by flexible generation, storage, demand response, or imports from neighbouring regions.

The duck curve describes the shape of net demand on sunny days: net load drops sharply at midday when solar output peaks, then ramps up steeply in the evening as the sun sets and household demand rises.

## Capacity factor

Capacity factor is the ratio of the energy a plant actually produces over a year to the energy it would produce running at full nameplate capacity all year. In this sample dataset, utility-scale solar plants have capacity factors of around 25 percent, onshore wind around 35 percent, and offshore wind around 45 percent.

## Curtailment

Curtailment happens when renewable generators are told to reduce output even though the wind or sun is available, usually because of transmission congestion or because supply exceeds demand. Adding storage and building new transmission lines are the two most common ways to reduce curtailment.
