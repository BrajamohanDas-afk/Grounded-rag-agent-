# Solar Energy Basics

Photovoltaic (PV) cells convert sunlight directly into electricity using semiconductor materials, most commonly silicon. When photons strike the cell, they knock electrons loose, creating an electric current.

Typical commercial silicon panels have an efficiency between 18 and 23 percent. Laboratory multi-junction cells have exceeded 47 percent efficiency under concentrated sunlight.

Solar panel output degrades slowly over time. Most manufacturers guarantee that panels will still produce at least 80 percent of their rated power after 25 years.

An inverter is required to convert the direct current (DC) produced by panels into alternating current (AC) used by household appliances and the grid.
